Project Overview

I developed this eight-page research and investigation packet for tenth-grade Physical Science students to examine the electricity and water demands of AI data centers. Students research consumption, compare electricity generation methods, and investigate the resources needed to cool computing equipment.

The assignment connects Physical Science with environmental sustainability, technological development, and real-world decision-making. Students use calculations and scientific information to put resource use into perspective and recommend ways to meet growing electricity needs.

Learning Objectives

I designed the questions to guide students as they:

  • Research and evaluate reliable scientific information and record their sources.
  • Apply mathematical reasoning to electricity and water consumption data using consistent time periods.
  • Compare fossil fuel, renewable, and nuclear electricity generation methods.
  • Evaluate energy sources for reliability, benefits, limitations, and environmental impacts.
  • Investigate data center cooling systems, water sources, and pressures on local water supplies.
  • Develop and defend recommendations using research evidence while acknowledging limitations.

Instructional Design

I organized the packet into three components. Structured questions move from finding and recording information to calculating comparisons, evaluating alternatives, and supporting a recommendation. Prompts for units, time periods, sources, and explanations give students a framework for working through complex scientific and environmental questions.

⚡

Electricity Consumption: How Much Is That Really?

Students research the electricity use of an Ohio data center and a familiar user, such as a household, school, electric vehicle, or refrigerator. They record consumption in kilowatt-hours and identify the time period and source for each figure.

The packet directs students to use the same time period for both values, calculate a ratio, and explain the result in everyday terms. This sequence gives large consumption figures a concrete point of comparison.

↔

Can We Produce Enough Electricity for AI?

Students investigate growing demand and research three electricity sources: a fossil fuel option, a renewable option, and nuclear power. Questions address how electricity is generated, operating conditions, direct carbon dioxide emissions, benefits, and challenges.

Students then compare reliability, environmental impacts, and the difficulties of expanding production. They recommend a source or combination of sources, support their choice with two specific facts from their research, and acknowledge a limitation of their recommendation.

≈

Water Consumption: Where Does It All Go?

Students explain how water is used in data center cooling, research a consumption figure, and compare it with another activity or industry. They calculate a ratio using the same time period and explain what it means.

The questions extend the investigation to potable water, reclaimed or recycled water, differences between cooling systems, and local supplies. Students research a way to reduce freshwater demand and identify both its potential benefit and a challenge associated with using it.

Implementation

The packet provides a sequence for classroom use. Students begin by researching and calculating an electricity-use comparison, then investigate generation options and support a recommendation with evidence. They finish by comparing water consumption and examining ways to reduce freshwater demand.

Responses are recorded within the structured packet, with spaces for sources, units, time periods, calculations, and explanations.

Reflection & Future Improvements

Overall, the AI Data Center investigation has been a positive learning experience, with many students nearing completion of the packet. Students have demonstrated a growing understanding of the electricity and water demands associated with artificial intelligence, particularly as they investigate common misconceptions surrounding the environmental impacts of emerging technologies.

One of the most successful components of the assignment has been the curated resource bank I developed to help students locate reliable information. Providing accessible, credible sources has allowed students to focus more on understanding and evaluating scientific information rather than struggling to identify trustworthy websites.

While student engagement has varied, many students appear to recognize the value of investigating emerging technologies beyond the headlines. The assignment has encouraged students to question simplified claims about AI's environmental impact and consider the complexities involved in electricity production, water consumption, and technological development.

One challenge has been the difficulty students experienced when researching and comparing electricity and water consumption figures. Finding reliable, comparable data and interpreting differences in units and time periods proved more demanding than I initially anticipated for this grade level.

In future implementations, I would provide additional scaffolding for these quantitative comparisons, including worked examples, more structured research guidance, and additional support for interpreting consumption data.

This experience has reinforced the importance of balancing challenging, real-world investigations with appropriate instructional support.
